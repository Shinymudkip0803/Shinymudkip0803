# Weekly Journal Entry Guide

## How to Approach Your Reflection

Each week, you'll reflect on course material using **one of three approaches**. Pick the one that connects most to what you're thinking about this week.

---

## The Three Approaches

### 🔗 The Connector
**Definition:** Link this week's material to something bigger—your project, a previous concept, real-world examples, or your own experience.

**Example Prompt:** "How does this week's reading connect to what I'm working on?"

---

### 🔧 The Troubleshooter
**Definition:** Document a challenge you faced and how you worked through it—technical, conceptual, or practical.

**Example Prompt:** "What was difficult this week, and how did I solve it?"

---

### 🎯 The Critic
**Definition:** Ask a critical question about the material. Challenge an assumption, identify a gap, or explore an implication.

**Example Prompt:** "What question or assumption from this week intrigues or concerns me?"

---

## Requirements

- **Length:** 250-300 words
- **Due:** Every Friday by 11:59 PM
- **Choose ONE approach** and delete the other two
- **Tag:** Include `#journal #weekX` at the bottom

# Week 2: Chapter 2 thoughts

**Date:** 01/23/2026
**Phase:** Journalist
**Approach Used:** Critic

## Your Reflection

The book talks about the "replication crisis", and how only 36% of tests were able to be replicated due to info being lost. But it does not say whether the scientists who performed the original tests, if alive, knew how to perform them, or did the researchers attempting to recreate the tests not try to reach out to contact them?  If Mass Communications is in a similar problem, then why not add regulations or requirements that need to be followed? If codebooks or data cleaning processes are getting lost, then why not require them to be documented and easy to access? If there is a data set on something like the age demographic of a product or service, then why not have a separate document that documents the steps taken to clean through and sort the data points; it would help with preventing the loss of data and knowledge. More often than not, if the how is not provided, then usually, the end product matters little. Because, what is the point of the end product if it cant be replicated or the reader has no idea how the answer got to what it is, since having no way to know means it could be considered useless information without any proof. Just like back in math classes, you need to show your work or the answer does not matter whether or not it is right or wrong. Preservation of methods *and* final product are equally as important at the end of the day.
