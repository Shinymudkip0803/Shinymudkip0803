# Weekly Journal Entry Guide

## How to Approach Your Reflection

Each week, you'll reflect on course material using **one of three approaches**. Pick the one that connects most to what you're thinking about this week.

---

## The Three Approaches

### 🔗 The Connector
**Definition:** Link this week's material to something bigger—your project, a previous concept, real-world examples, or your own experience.

**Example Prompt:** "How does this week's reading connect to what I'm working on?"

---

### 🔧 The Troubleshooter
**Definition:** Document a challenge you faced and how you worked through it—technical, conceptual, or practical.

**Example Prompt:** "What was difficult this week, and how did I solve it?"

---

### 🎯 The Critic
**Definition:** Ask a critical question about the material. Challenge an assumption, identify a gap, or explore an implication.

**Example Prompt:** "What question or assumption from this week intrigues or concerns me?"

---

## Requirements

- **Length:** 250-300 words
- **Due:** Every Friday by 11:59 PM
- **Choose ONE approach** and delete the other two
- **Tag:** Include `#journal #weekX` at the bottom

# Week 3: Chapter 3

**Date:** 1/30/2026
**Phase:** Journalist
**Approach Used:** Critic

## Your Reflection

Authority based knowledge is flawed in its very initial concepts. By deciding to simply trust what your higher ups tell you, is a quick way to be given wrong info that could cause a myriad of different problems. Intuition can also be innately wrong, just because something feels right, does not mean it is guaranteed to be even slightly correct. The chapter itself even states that it is "vulnerable to cognitive biases." Empiricism is consistant but slow, while it does work well with it changing based on gathered info every experiment. Having to perform an experiment each time takes a long time before you may have something resembling a final product. Falsifiability is a fairly bare-bones method. There is little experimenting to be done, it is mostly just taking surveys and looking at the data which, does not make for a more concrete product. Yes, Falsifiability is important, but I do not think it should be the main method used for experimentation. Inductive reasoning is a good way to get a general idea of something, but, not very helpful when it comes to specifics or minor details. Due to it using patterns you likely would not be able to replicate it at all either. Deductive reasoning is too confined in its ways, if you have a theory, the method deems that it must be correct or the research was pointless. Most of this chapter is about research methods, and all of them have flaws even if some are less flawed than others appear to be.
